## Abstract

Facial image retrieval in unconstrained surveillance environments is a high-stakes challenge where missing a subject of interest—a single false negative—is simply not an option. Despite near-perfect performance on curated benchmarks, current recognition systems falter under real-world domain shifts such as low resolution, motion blur, and uncontrolled illumination (e.g., SCFace). Addressing this reliability gap, we propose **Risk-Aware Facial Retrieval (RA-FR)**, a framework that moves beyond fixed Top-$k$ retrieval to adaptive set generation, guaranteeing ground truth inclusion within a user-specified risk level ($\alpha$) and confidence level ($1-\delta$). Our approach integrates three core contributions: (1) reducing aleatoric uncertainty via a hybrid blind face restoration technique coupling Latent Consistency Models (InterLCM) and DiffBIR; (2) extracting discriminative, restoration-robust features via self-supervised DINOv1 ViT-B with GGeM pooling; and (3) employing conformal prediction with Hoeffding's inequality to dynamically calibrate retrieval set sizes based on query uncertainty. On the IMFDB benchmark, it consistently satisfies a **5\% risk target** with an average retrieval set size of approximately **10 images**. By unifying domain-specific restoration, robust representation learning, and provable decision rules, RA-FR offers a pipeline that makes facial retrieval in surveillance both reliable and auditable.

</HighlightedSection>

## Motivation

Face retrieval is increasingly deployed in security, surveillance, and identity verification. Yet most systems treat every prediction with equal confidence, making them vulnerable in safety-critical settings. A single high-confidence but incorrect match can have severe consequences. **RA-FR** addresses this gap by making the retrieval system *risk-aware*, quantifying and communicating uncertainty so that downstream decision-makers can act accordingly.

## Method

### Model Architectures

We evaluate three architectures that pair a visual backbone with a pooling strategy suited to retrieval:

| Architecture | Backbone | Pooling | Role |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| R50-GeM | ResNet-50 | Generalized Mean (GeM) | Baseline |
| ViTB-DN-GeM | ViT-Base + DINO | GeM | Proposed |
| ViTB-DN-GGeM | ViT-Base + DINO | Group GeM | Proposed (Main) |

The **DINO**-pretrained ViT-Base backbone provides strong spatial representations learned through self-supervised distillation. **Group GeM** extends standard GeM by applying generalized mean pooling across groups of feature channels, capturing richer multi-scale information for retrieval.

### Uncertainty Estimation

We employ two complementary uncertainty estimation approaches:

- **Bayesian Transfer Learning (BTL):** Places a Bayesian last layer on top of the frozen or fine-tuned feature extractor, enabling closed-form posterior inference over retrieval scores without costly MCMC sampling.
- **Monte Carlo Dropout (MCD):** Activates dropout at test time and performs $T$ stochastic forward passes. The variance across the resulting embedding distributions serves as the uncertainty estimate: $\sigma^2 = \frac{1}{T} \sum_{t=1}^{T} \| f_t(x) - \bar{f}(x) \|^2$

### Risk-Aware Retrieval Pipeline

The RA-FR pipeline augments standard retrieval with an uncertainty-driven decision layer:

1. **Embed** the query face using the trained backbone + pooling.
2. **Retrieve** nearest neighbors from the gallery via cosine similarity.
3. **Estimate uncertainty** of the retrieval using BTL or MCD.
4. **Flag** retrievals whose uncertainty exceeds a calibrated threshold, enabling operators to request manual verification instead of trusting an unreliable match.

Key findings from the calibration analysis:

- **ViTB-DN-GeM achieves the best calibration** on IMFDB with BTL (ECE = 0.245) and MCD (ECE = 0.059), closely tracking the ideal diagonal.
- **R50-GeM struggles on SCFace**, where ECE degrades to 0.485 (BTL) and 0.482 (MCD), reflecting poor generalization to surveillance conditions.
- **MCD produces tighter uncertainty distributions** compared to BTL, especially for the ViT-based architectures.
- The proposed architectures exhibit **higher density at lower uncertainty values** on IMFDB, indicating more confident and better-calibrated in-domain retrieval.

The cross-domain evaluation from IMFDB → SCFace simulates a realistic deployment scenario, where training data comes from controlled movie scenes but the system must handle low-resolution surveillance footage. The proposed ViT-based architectures demonstrate substantially stronger generalization, maintaining both retrieval accuracy and calibration quality under this challenging domain shift.

## Key Takeaways

- **Uncertainty matters for trust.** Integrating uncertainty quantification into face retrieval transforms a black-box system into one that can communicate reliability, which is essential for safety-critical applications.
- **ViT + DINO features excel at calibration.** Self-supervised ViT representations produce better-calibrated uncertainty estimates than supervised ResNet features, especially under domain shift.
- **MCD and BTL are complementary.** MCD tends to produce tighter, better-separated uncertainty distributions, while BTL provides computationally cheaper estimates with competitive calibration.
- **Group GeM improves retrieval capacity.** The ViTB-DN-GGeM architecture achieves the strongest raw retrieval performance, extending the standard GeM with group-wise pooling over ViT features.
